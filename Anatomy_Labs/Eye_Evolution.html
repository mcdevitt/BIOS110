1. Retina Wiring & Blind Spot (The "Smoking Gun")

Human (Vertebrate)

Inverted Retina: Photoreceptors point away from incoming light. Ganglion nerve fibers run across the front of the retina and bundle together to exit through the optic disc, creating a blind spot where no photoreceptors can sit.

Octopus (Cephalopod)

Everted Retina: Photoreceptors point toward incoming light. Nerve fibers route directly out the back of the retina into the optic lobe, producing a continuous visual field with no blind spot.

Why this proves convergent evolution: Natural selection builds on existing developmental layouts rather than redesigning from scratch. Because vertebrate and cephalopod eyes arose independently, they arrived at opposite wiring solutions for the exact same optical problem.

Module 2 · Phylogeny & Evo-Devo

Evolutionary Tree: Convergence vs. Deep Homology

Click any lineage or ancestor node on the cladogram to trace how complex eyes evolved independently across animal phyla while sharing an ancient genetic toolkit (Pax6 & Opsin).

The "Deep Homology" Breakthrough (Walter Gehring, 1995): When scientists inserted the mouse Pax6 gene into a developing fruit fly's leg or antenna, it triggered the growth of a functional fly compound eye—not a mouse eye! Pax6 is the ancient master switch inherited from our common ancestor, while downstream genes build each lineage's distinct eye architecture.
Ancestral Node · ~580 Million Years Ago

Urbilaterian Last Common Ancestor

Master Gene: Ancestral Pax6 Photopigment: Opsins

The last common ancestor of vertebrates, octopuses, and insects was a small marine worm-like bilaterian. It did not possess a camera eye or a lens. Instead, it had simple dermal eyespots (clusters of photoreceptor cells paired with screening pigment) capable only of sensing light versus shadow.

What Was Inherited vs. What Evolved Later

Inherited Toolkit (Homologous): Light-sensitive opsin proteins coupled to G-proteins, screening pigments, and the Pax6 transcription factor that initiates eye development.

Evolved Independently (Analogous): Corneas, focusing lenses, irises, and image-forming retinas evolved separately after the bilaterian lineages diverged.

Module 3 · Gradual Morphological Evolution

How a Camera Eye Evolves Step-by-Step

In 1994, Dan-Erik Nilsson and Susanne Pelger modeled how a flat patch of light-sensitive skin can evolve into a focused camera eye in ~364,000 generations—with every intermediate stage offering an immediate survival advantage.

~0 Generations (Ancestral Starting Point)
Morphological Cross-Section Unfocused Ambient Light

Stage 1: Flat Photosensitive Epithelium

A simple flat patch of light-sensitive cells backed by dark screening pigment. It tells the organism whether it is day or night, or if a predator's shadow passes overhead, but cannot detect which direction light is coming from.

Spatial Resolution (Image Sharpness) 2% — Light vs. Dark Only
Light-Gathering Sensitivity (Brightness) 85% — Wide Open
Selection Advantage at This Step: Detects overhead shadows and circadian light cycles without needing complex neural wiring.
Living Modern Analogue
Starfish Tube-Foot Tips & Earthworm Epidermis

Many invertebrates retain simple flat eyespots because basic shadow detection is sufficient for burrowing or slow benthic lifestyles.
