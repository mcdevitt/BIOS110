Run Progress: 0% (Loaded in Wells) Voltage: 100 V
  • Lane 1 — DNA Ladder (4000 bp to 100 bp): Contains DNA fragments of known sizes. Notice how smaller fragments (100–300 bp) weave rapidly through agarose pores toward the bottom, while large fragments (2000–4000 bp) stay near the top.
  • Lane 2 — Genomic DNA (gDNA) Sample: Extracted chromosomal DNA is huge and sheared into many overlapping sizes during extraction, producing a continuous vertical smear rather than one single band.
  • Lane 3 — PCR Product Sample (~600 bp): PCR copies only one specific target region of exact length (~600 bp), producing a single, sharp band located between the 500 bp and 800 bp ladder markers.
Worksheet Section 1 • Overview Concepts

Overview — Why Are We Using Gel Electrophoresis Today?

Click each question below to reveal the key takeaway for your worksheet.

DNA extraction was successful—specifically whether genomic DNA (gDNA) is present in our sample, how much DNA was recovered (indicated by smear brightness/thickness), and whether the DNA is intact high-molecular-weight DNA or heavily degraded.
PCR amplification successfully produced the expected target DNA fragment (a sharp band at ~600 bp), how strongly the target amplified, and whether our positive control worked while our negative control remained free of contamination.
DNA has a negatively charged phosphate backbone, so when an electric current is applied, DNA migrates through the gel from the negative cathode (−) toward the positive anode (+). The agarose gel forms a microscopic porous matrix (molecular sieve): smaller DNA fragments slip through the pores with less friction and resistance, allowing them to travel faster and farther down the gel than larger fragments.
