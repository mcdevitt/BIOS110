Key Rule

Arteries carry blood away from the heart; veins carry blood toward the heart. That is why the pulmonary artery carries O₂-poor blood (40/45) and the pulmonary vein carries O₂-rich blood (104/40)!

Large Elastic Arteries (Aorta)

Anatomical Structure

Thick walls rich in elastic fibers and smooth muscle; wide lumen; no valves.

Physiological Function

Stretches during ventricular systole and elastically recoils during diastole to maintain continuous forward blood flow.

Why it matters on the graph: Elastic recoil keeps diastolic pressure around 80 mm Hg even when the heart relaxes, propelling blood at high velocity (~40 cm/sec) toward the organs.

1. Lungs • Alveolar Gas Exchange Diaphragm Contracts → Air In

Alveolus ↔ Pulmonary Capillary

O₂ and CO₂ are small, nonpolar molecules that cross the 1-cell-thick alveolar epithelium and capillary endothelium by simple diffusion (no ATP required) down their concentration gradients.

Structure → Function Connection: Why are alveoli grape-like clusters surrounded by dense capillary nets? Millions of alveoli create a massive surface area (~70 m²), and walls only 1 cell thick keep diffusion distance tiny—allowing O₂ to load onto hemoglobin (4 O₂ per Hb) in less than 1 second!
2. Tissues • Cellular Respiration Link Gradient: Normal

Systemic Capillary ↔ Body Cell & Mitochondrion

Mitochondrial cellular respiration consumes O₂ to make ATP and releases CO₂ as waste—maintaining the low tissue PO₂ (<40) and high PCO₂ (>45) that drive diffusion across membranes.

Big-Picture Synthesis: Breathing and circulation exist to serve cellular respiration! Mitochondria pull O₂ into cells by constantly using it to make ATP, and push CO₂ out into the blood by breaking down fuel molecules.
