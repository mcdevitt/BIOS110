Control: Coupled Oxidative Phosphorylation

Electrons from NADH (Complex I) and FADH₂ (Complex II) travel down the ETC to O₂, pumping H⁺ into the intermembrane space. Because the inner membrane is impermeable to ions, H⁺ can only return to the matrix through ATP Synthase, coupling electron transport directly to ATP production.

Key Takeaway: Electron transport and ATP synthesis are separate processes linked solely by the H⁺ gradient!

Protein Catabolism: Deamination

Endurance / Starvation

Amino acids are normally saved for building proteins, but during prolonged fasting or endurance exercise, enzymes strip off their nitrogen-containing amino group (-NH₂ → NH₃ → excreted as Urea). The remaining carbon skeletons enter at Pyruvate, Acetyl-CoA, or Krebs intermediates.

Hormonal Gatekeeping

Insulin & GLUT4 Translocation

Insulin Absent (Fasting): GLUT4 transporters remain sequestered inside cytoplasmic vesicles. Hydrophilic glucose cannot cross the lipid bilayer and stays in the bloodstream.

Allosteric Pacemaker

PFK Dual-Site Regulation

Cell Energy State: Balanced ATP / ADP
1. Rest (High ATP) 2. Normal 3. Active (High ADP/AMP)

Dual-Site Logic: ATP is both a substrate at PFK's active site and an allosteric inhibitor at high concentrations. ADP/AMP bind the allosteric site to activate PFK when energy runs low.

Immediate Sprint Buffer ~7-Sec ATP Reserve

Phosphocreatine & Creatine Kinase

0–2 Seconds: Resting muscle stores only enough free ATP for ~7 seconds of maximal effort. As myosin heads hydrolyze ATP → ADP, Creatine Kinase immediately transfers Pᵢ from Phosphocreatine to ADP (substrate-level phosphorylation).
