In the lab manual crime scene scenario, investigators recovered a tiny drop of blood on a broken glass at the scene. Both Suspect 1 and Suspect 2 had circumstantial ties—motive, opportunity, or documented access to the location—requiring forensic DNA profiling to determine whether either individual was the source of the bloodstain.

Suspect 1 (S1 / Lane 1) Had direct access to the room/glass prior to the incident and lacked a verified alibi during the time window of the crime, placing them on the investigator suspect list.
Suspect 2 (S2 / Lane 2) Had a known dispute/motive and opportunity near the crime scene, making a reference DNA sample necessary to include or exclude them against the crime scene blood.

Why Did the Lab Technician Need to Use PCR on the Tiny Blood Sample?

The microscopic blood spot recovered from the glass contained only trace nanogram or picogram quantities of genomic DNA—far too little DNA to fluoresce visibly on an agarose gel after electrophoresis.

Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR) solves this limitation by using sequence-specific primers flanking the STR region to exponentially amplify that target locus across ~30 thermal cycles, generating millions to billions of identical DNA copies that produce bright, sharp bands on the gel.

What Is a Short Tandem Repeat (STR)?

A Short Tandem Repeat (STR) is a short DNA sequence motif (typically 2 to 6 base pairs long, such as GATA or AGAT) repeated head-to-tail (in tandem) within non-coding regions of human chromosomes.

Because the number of tandem repeats at a given STR locus varies widely among unrelated individuals—and because each person inherits one allele from each parent—PCR amplification produces distinct DNA fragment lengths (1 band if homozygous, 2 bands if heterozygous) that separate by size during gel electrophoresis.

What Are 3 Advantages of Using STRs for DNA Fingerprinting?

  • 1. High Discrimination Power STR loci are highly polymorphic with many possible repeat-length alleles in the population. Analyzing multiple independent STR loci together makes a coincidental match between two unrelated people astronomically unlikely.
  • 2. Ideal for Degraded & Trace DNA Because STR amplicons are relatively small (~100–400 bp), PCR can reliably amplify intact STR regions even from old, environmentally damaged, or tiny crime scene stains where long DNA fragments would be broken.
  • 3. Standardized & Reproducible Multiplexing Multiple core STR loci (such as the CODIS panel) can be amplified and compared reliably across laboratories using standardized primers, ladders, and gel or capillary electrophoresis.

Stage 4 • All STRs Together Pending
Considering STR 1, STR 2, and STR 3 together, does the crime scene DNA profile match either suspect?

Does the profile match either suspect? No. In forensic DNA analysis, a true match requires identical allele band patterns across every tested locus. Even though Suspect 1 matched STR 1 and STR 2 by coincidence/shared common alleles, the clear mismatch at STR 3 completely excludes Suspect 1 (and Suspect 2 was excluded at all three loci).

What does the data suggest? The blood sample at the crime scene was left by an unknown third individual who has not yet been sampled. This demonstrates why forensic scientists must test multiple STR loci rather than relying on a single marker.
