Worksheet Part 1: Why Each DNA Extraction Step Is Important

Test yourself first, then click each button to verify your worksheet table responses.

Step Importance / Purpose in DNA Extraction Check
Lysis
Breaks open bacterial cell walls and phospholipid plasma membranes (via lysozyme, bead beating, and Lysis Buffers A & B) to release genomic DNA into solution while denaturing nuclease enzymes that would otherwise degrade DNA.
Binding
Under high-salt binding buffer conditions, negatively charged DNA selectively attaches to the white silica membrane inside the spin column while cellular debris and unwanted molecules pass through into the waste tube.
Washing
Removes remaining proteins, lipids, carbohydrates, and inhibitory salts (using 1st & 2nd Wash Buffers plus a 2-minute ethanol dry spin) while keeping the DNA tightly bound to the silica membrane.
Elution
Uses low-salt Elution Buffer (20 µL, soaked for 2 min) to reverse silica-DNA interactions, releasing purified DNA off the membrane and collecting it in the flow-through inside the yellow 1.5 mL tube.
gDNA
Provides purified, concentrated bacterial genomic DNA (the organism's complete set of genetic material) free of PCR inhibitors, ready for downstream amplification, gel electrophoresis, and DNA sequencing.

Guided Worksheet Lab Notes (Parts 3–8 Reflections)

Use these key observations and scientific explanations when completing your lab notebook reflections:

Part 3 — Cell Lysis Reflection

What observations give you confidence you completed cell lysis correctly?

Key Indicators: After spinning the BashingBead tube at 12,000 RPM, a tight bead/debris pellet formed at the bottom and only clear supernatant (400 µL) was transferred. After adding 600 µL Lysis Buffer A + 600 µL Lysis Buffer B and mixing slowly without bubbles, the lysate became clear throughout (or cleared after 1–3 min warming if precipitate formed).

Part 4 — DNA Binding Reflection

What did you pay the most attention to during the DNA binding step?

Key Indicators: Loading a maximum of 800 µL per spin (twice, to pass all ~1,600 µL lysate), resting the pipette tip against the side wall so it never punctured the delicate white silica membrane, spinning at 12,000 RPM for 1 min until all liquid passed into the "Lysis Buffer Waste" tube, and emptying waste into the liquid waste container.

Parts 5 & 6 — Washing Reflection

How confident are you that the DNA was washed correctly?

Key Indicators: Added 200 µL of 1st Wash Buffer (Blue) first, spun and emptied waste; then added 500 µL of 2nd Wash Buffer (Gray), spun and emptied waste; and completed the mandatory 2-minute dry spin at 12,000 RPM to evaporate residual ethanol that would otherwise inhibit PCR next week.

Part 7 — DNA Elution Reflection

How confident are you that the DNA was successfully released from the silica membrane?

Key Indicators: Transferred the All-White DNA Spin Column into the labeled yellow 1.5 mL tube, dispensed 20 µL of Pink Elution Buffer directly onto the center of the silica membrane using the black pipette, let it soak for a full 2 minutes before centrifuging at 12,000 RPM, and saved the final flow-through in the yellow tube.

Part 8 — Pure gDNA Extract & Looking Ahead

Do you think you will see your gDNA extract in upcoming labs? Why?

Key Indicators: Yes—if lysis released sufficient bacterial DNA, the silica membrane remained intact during binding/washing, ethanol was removed during the dry spin, and the 20 µL elution flow-through was saved in your section's gDNA storage box (A1–J9), you will see a high-molecular-weight gDNA smear on your gel and a sharp amplicon band after PCR.
