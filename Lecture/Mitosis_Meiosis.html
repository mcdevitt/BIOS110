Cyclin Oscillation & MPF (Maturation-Promoting Factor) Wave

CDK protein levels stay constant; Cyclins are synthesized and degraded via ubiquitin-proteasome pathways to drive phase transitions.

G₂ → M (MPF Peak)
G₂ → M Transition: MPF Peak

Accumulated G₂ Cyclin (Cyclin B) binds to constant CDK to form active MPF. MPF phosphorylates nuclear lamins (dissolving the nuclear envelope), condensins, and spindle assembly proteins to launch mitosis.

Key Exam Concept: Destruction of G₂ cyclin inactivates MPF, allowing the cell to complete Anaphase/Telophase and return to G₁.

Maternal Age & Oogenesis Prophase I Arrest

Study Guide Q

Why does a 42-year-old woman carry a higher risk of trisomy (e.g., Trisomy 21 / Down syndrome) than a 22-year-old woman or a 42-year-old man? Drag the maternal age slider to inspect oocyte cohesion integrity:

Maternal Age at Ovulation: 22 yrs Low Nondisjunction Risk (~1/1,500)
Fetal Prophase I Arrest (Age 0) Age 25 Age 35 Age 45

Oogenesis Mechanism: Primary oocytes enter Meiosis I during fetal development and arrest in Prophase I for decades until ovulation. Over 35–40+ years, chromosomal cohesin proteins holding homologous chiasmata and sister chromatids gradually degrade, increasing Anaphase I/II nondisjunction.

Why Older Men Have Lower Aneuploidy Risk: Spermatogenesis begins at puberty and produces fresh sperm continuously (~64–74 days from stem cell to mature sperm) without a multi-decade Prophase I arrest.
