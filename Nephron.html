Why "Dump Everything & Reabsorb Selectively"?

Filtering out thousands of distinct toxins individually would require thousands of bespoke molecular pumps. Instead, the mammalian nephron uses a two-stage systems design: bulk pressure-driven filtration at the glomerulus followed by coupled active/passive recovery along specialized segments spanning the Cortex (300 mOsm) and Medulla (up to 1200 mOsm).

Key Systems Principle

Water reabsorption in the nephron is always passive (via osmosis through aquaporins). Therefore, it requires two simultaneous conditions: (1) an external osmotic gradient built by active salt pumping, and (2) membrane permeability (aquaporins).
