Patellar Tendon Stretch & Receptor Depolarization

1. Nervous System Division
Peripheral Nervous System (PNS) → Sensory (Afferent) Division
2. Anatomical Structure
Patellar Tendon Muscle Spindle → Femoral Nerve → Dorsal Root Ganglion
3. Cell & Glial Support Type
Pseudounipolar Sensory Neuron + Schwann Cell Myelin Sheath (PNS)
4. Cellular & Synaptic Mechanism

Mechanical stretch opens mechanosensitive cation channels, depolarizing the receptor past -55 mV threshold. Voltage-gated Na⁺ channels snap open (+40 mV spike), triggering rapid saltatory conduction across Nodes of Ranvier into the spinal cord.

CNS Brain + Spinal Cord
Central Nervous System

Integration hub. High Five uses Frontal Lobe, Motor Cortex & Cerebellum; Knee Reflex integrates solely in Spinal Cord gray matter. Myelinated by Oligodendrocytes.

PNS • AFFERENT Sensory Input
Sensory (Afferent) Division

Carries signals into the CNS. Carries tendon stretch in the Knee Reflex, and visual + joint proprioception feedback during a High Five.

PNS • SOMATIC Skeletal Muscle
Somatic Motor (Efferent)

Drives both the High Five (voluntary arm muscles) and the Knee Reflex (involuntary quadriceps contraction) via acetylcholine (ACh) at neuromuscular junctions.

PNS • AUTONOMIC Smooth/Cardiac/Glands
Sympathetic & Parasympathetic

Involuntary internal homeostasis (e.g., thermoregulation via sweat glands & blood vessels, heart rate). Sympathetic = Fight-or-Flight; Parasympathetic = Rest-and-Digest.
